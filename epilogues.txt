THE GROUND
The vote is closer than anyone expected, and then it isn't. The Hollow Rider Co-op is chartered by spring. Ruth chairs the board and runs it like a seminar. Mo's yard becomes a training bay, and Teo, sixteen and insufferable, drives the Saturday loop with a licensed instructor and his own hand-drawn sign: BOUNCE.
The loop runs. Stops are added where people asked for them, not where a model said. Eli's ledger is framed in the depot office; underneath it, a new one. Everyone is still here.
On the last page, in pencil, in your handwriting: It only bounces if you let it hit the ground.

THE LONG WAY
The hybrid plan passes by one vote. The express meets the loop at the Square, and for the first time in thirty years a bus runs from the Hollow's last porch to the valley hospital without a transfer. Greer calls it the least efficient efficient plan she ever drafted.
Ruth gets a door-to-door pilot. Hal's diner sells coffee to forty valley commuters a day, and he complains about it with real joy. Bounce keeps the loop, with a county contract and a modest, reliable wage.
You and Greer have standing Tuesday coffee. Nobody ever says what it is.

SCALE
The check clears on a Friday. By spring, Stride's blue-and-white fleet runs the valley: electric, quiet, on time, and exactly as warm as a spreadsheet. The Hollow gets a better schedule and a worse goodbye.
Bounce is retired to a glass case in Stride's regional office. A plaque says "Heritage Vehicle." Ruth sends a card. Teo sends a drawing of the bus with a ball on top. Hal sends nothing, which says the most.
You have money, a view, and a quiet apartment. In the glovebox of your new car there is a red rubber ball. You haven't dropped it yet.

DETOUR
Mo buys the bus for the price of the loan, and drives it himself. He says he's keeping it warm. You take the long road out of the valley with a duffel bag, a ball, and the sense of a door held open behind you.
Years later, on a different road, you stop at a gas station and watch a red ball roll out of a kid's hand. It bounces. It bounces again. The kid looks up, grinning, as if the ground were a promise someone finally kept.
You wonder how far the Hollow is from here. Then you get back in the car.
